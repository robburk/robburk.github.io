THE OPPORTUNITY

USDA COMMUNITY FACILITIES FUNDING

The USDA Community Facilities Direct Loan & Grant Program funds the essential public projects rural towns struggle to finance on their own — town halls, highway garages, fire and EMS stations, libraries, senior centers, public safety vehicles, and equipment.

Grants cover 15 to 75 percent of a project's cost, with low-interest USDA loans available for the balance. Towns with smaller populations and lower median incomes qualify for the highest grant percentages. Applications are accepted year-round — there is no deadline to race against.

It is one of the most useful funding programs available to small municipalities, and one of the most underused, because the application work is substantial and most small towns have no one on staff to do it.

HOW I WORK

NO FEE UNLESS YOU'RE FUNDED.

I work on a pure contingency basis: no upfront cost, no retainer, no hourly billing. If a grant is awarded, my fee is a fixed percentage of the award, paid at closing. If the application isn't funded, the town owes nothing.

I'm able to offer this because I've built a faster way to handle the document-heavy parts of an application — requirement analysis, drafting, budget formatting, and compliance language. That efficiency is what lets me take a town's application on contingency when a traditional grant consultant has to charge upfront. The town always remains the applicant of record; I do the heavy lifting, and you review and approve every word before anything is submitted.

WHO THIS IS FOR

RURAL TOWNS AND VILLAGES.

USDA Community Facilities funding is available to towns, villages, and special districts with populations under 20,000, located in eligible rural areas. Most of the Hudson Valley and Catskills qualifies. If you're not sure whether your community is eligible, that's the first thing I'll check — at no cost.
